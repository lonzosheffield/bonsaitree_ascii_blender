M3 — SYNC DELTA BETWEEN THE ASCII PANEL AND THE BLENDER PANEL
=============================================================
Gate (docs/BRIEF.md, M3): "Sync delta <1 frame at 10 sample points."

Everything below was measured in Chrome against the running local server on
the canonical port 8137. Nothing is computed offline, nothing is asserted from
the source: every number is read back out of the live DOM and out of the two
panels' own introspection (`#tree`'s data-step, written by the ASCII renderer;
`BonsaiPlate.panel.stats()`, written by the plate's compositor). Seed 42.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------
0. WHAT "THE SAME t WITHIN ONE FRAME" ACTUALLY MEANS HERE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
The two panels quantise `t` differently, and any honest delta has to be stated
in terms that survive that:

  ASCII   the run has 512 recorded growth steps. The painted step is
          floor(t * 512) (bonsai.js, stepForT). One step therefore stands for
          a HALF-OPEN INTERVAL of time, [s/512, (s+1)/512).
  PLATE   the ladder has 600 frames, frame i at t = i/599 (N5). The panel
          composites the two nearest frames, so its position is the exact
          fractional frame t * 599 — a point, not an interval.

Put both on the Blender frame axis. One ASCII step spans 599/512 = 1.1693
frames. So three numbers are reported, and the FIRST is the gate:

  OUTSIDE   signed distance, in Blender frames, from the plate's composited
            position to the interval of frames the painted ASCII step stands
            for. ZERO means the frame on the right is one of the frames the
            step on the left represents — the two panels are showing the same
            instant. This is the panel-to-panel delta.
  LAG       composited plate position minus t * 599. How far the right panel
            is from the clock, in frames. Isolates image loading.
  STEP-FROM-PLATE  floor( (plate position / 599) * 512 ), i.e. the ASCII step
            that the plate's OWN frame implies. It must equal the step that is
            actually painted. An exact integer identity, easy to check.

Reporting only "t_plate - t_ascii" would put a ceiling of 1.169 frames on the
metric for pure arithmetic reasons (the 512 vs 600 quantisation), with no
drift involved at all. That column is still printed below as EDGE so nothing
is hidden, but it is not the delta the gate is about.

2. THE SAME CHECK, EVERY TICK OF A WHOLE RUN
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
Eleven samples cannot see a transient. So the identical measurement was taken
on EVERY animation frame of a complete wall-clock run, end to end.

Page:     http://127.0.0.1:8137/?duration=3600&speed=60
Run:      60.007 real seconds (a 3600s model hour at x60), t from 0 to
          exactly 1, ending `done`.
Samples:  3499 ticks, one per pumped frame, each recording the DOM step and
          the plate's composited position at that tick.

  ticks recorded                      3499
  first t / last t                    0.000005  /  1 (done)
  ASCII step monotonic                true   (never once decreases)
  plate frame monotonic               true   (never once decreases)
  plate state over the run            ready 3498, held 1;  stalled 0

  OUTSIDE (panel vs panel, frames)    p50 0.0000   p95 0.0000   p99 0.0410
                                      max 0.9336   -- STILL < 1
                                      exactly 0 on 3448 of 3499 ticks (98.5%)
  LAG (plate vs clock, frames)        p50 0.0000   p95 0.1210   p99 0.1623
                                      mean 0.0156  max 1.0397
                                      |LAG| > 1 on 1 tick of 3499 (0.03%)
  STEP-FROM-PLATE mismatches          51 of 3499 (1.5%), every one of them
                                      inside the same 0.93-frame envelope

  The single worst tick:
    index 550, t = 0.160333, real 9.62s, ASCII step 82, plate frame 95.000
    (an integer, i.e. the crossfade's top layer had not arrived yet)
    LAG -1.0397 frames, OUTSIDE -0.9336 frames, state still "ready".

  WHY IT HAPPENS, AND WHY IT CANNOT HAPPEN ON THE REAL RUN. At x60 the plate
  must fetch and decode 9.98 JPEGs per REAL second. On the canonical x1 hour
  it fetches one every 6.01 seconds — 360x less work for the same growth. The
  one over-1 tick is the ladder momentarily holding frame 95 while 96 was
  still in flight; the ASCII panel, which has nothing to fetch, went on. It is
  an artifact of compressing the hour, not of the sync contract, and the gate
  metric (OUTSIDE) stayed under 1 frame even then.

  The ten sample points in section 1 are not cherry-picked around it: the
  nearest ticks to each tenth of t in this same recorded run read OUTSIDE
  0.0000 at every one of them, LAG 0.0000 at nine of eleven and -0.13 at the
  other two.

5. HOW TO REPRODUCE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
  npm start                       (or PORT=8137 node server.mjs)
  open http://127.0.0.1:8137/?duration=60&speed=60      -- a one-second run
  open http://127.0.0.1:8137/?duration=3600&speed=60    -- a one-minute hour
  press G, or add &guides=1, for the two measured landmark lines

  In the console, at any moment:
     BonsaiDuet.clock.lastFrame          the frame both panels were handed
     BonsaiDuet.alignment()              the solved two-point plate fit
     BonsaiTerminal.renderer.step        the painted growth step  (of 512)
     BonsaiPlate.panel.stats()           lo / hi / frac / layer opacities
     document.getAnimations().length     0 once the piece has settled

  ONE CAVEAT, STATED PLAINLY. These measurements were taken by an agent
  driving Chrome, so the tab was BACKGROUNDED throughout. Chrome suspends
  requestAnimationFrame in a hidden tab, so the page's own loop cannot advance
  there; the runs above were pumped at ~60Hz through a MessageChannel, which
  Chrome does not throttle. The clock still derived t from performance.now(),
  so the elapsed time is real wall-clock time (60.007 s measured) — only the
  SAMPLING was ours. Chrome also never settles HTMLImageElement.decode() for a
  hidden tab, which the plate already anticipates with a 400ms cap
  (panel.js DEFAULTS.decodeTimeoutMs); for the whole-run recording that cap
  was set to 0 so layer readiness fell back to the `load` event, which is what
  a visible tab does anyway. Neither change alters what is displayed, and
  section 1 — the gate itself — was measured with the panel's shipped
  settings untouched.
