M3 — THE BLOOM FINALE, AND HOW THE PIECE COMES TO REST
======================================================
docs/BRIEF.md: "at t=3600s both hold at full bloom and stop."
M4 will soak this for a real hour: "ends bloomed and **stopped**."

This file records what happens at t = 1, why it is as quiet as it is, and the
measurement that the page genuinely STOPS rather than merely looking finished.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE BRIEF SAYS "STOP". MOST WAYS OF ENDING A WEB PAGE DO NOT.
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
A CSS animation with `animation-fill-mode: forwards` holds its last keyframe,
which LOOKS like stopping. It is not: the animation stays alive on the
compositor forever, and so does every infinite one that was already running.
An hour-long piece that ends by parking a dozen live animations has not come
to rest, and the M4 soak would spend the rest of the hour measuring them.

So the ending is written in two stages, both driven from index.html's
`arrive()`, and each flag is written exactly once per page:

  body[data-state="held"]     the instant the clock reports t = 1.
                              Every finale rule in src/styles.css hangs off
                              this, and every animation it starts is a single
                              pass (`1 forwards`); every transition it starts
                              is self-terminating.
  body[data-settled="true"]   SETTLE_MS = 3200 ms later, once that pass is
                              over. It removes the animations outright and
                              writes the values they landed on directly, so
                              the final state is held by static CSS.

The second flag is also what silences the one thing that DOES loop during the
run: the terminal's phosphor breath on `.crt-mask`. A CRT breathing is alive;
a finished piece should not be.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. WHAT THE ARRIVAL ACTUALLY IS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
Restraint was the brief. This is the end of a slow hour, not a reveal, so
nothing moves, nothing scales, nothing flashes, and nothing appears that was
not already on the page. Over ~3 seconds, four things that are already there
take one breath of light and settle just above where they were:

  the rail        the BLOOM chip stops pulsing. The 2.4s pulse that ran
                  through the final phase — the approach signal — resolves
                  into one steady lit chip (`bloom-hold`, 2800ms, one pass).
                  The four phases already crossed step BACK a shade, so BLOOM
                  is unambiguously the state the piece ended in rather than
                  merely the last one lit.
  the terminal    its glow swells by about a third and returns to a little
                  above its running level (`arrive-terminal`, 3200ms).
  the plate       the same move in the studio's cool white, so both frames
                  breathe together (`arrive-plate`, 3200ms).
  the bar         the growing tip at the head of the fill becomes a finished
                  end cap (`arrive-cap`, 2600ms) at 100% of the track.

  Two quieter marks, as transitions rather than animations:
  the ground line and the two header counters settle a shade warmer — the
  only sign the panels themselves give that this is an end and not a pause.

  The readout's tag becomes "seed 42 - bloomed - held". It carries
  aria-live="polite", so across a whole hour a screen reader is told exactly
  two things: what run this is, and that it has arrived.

  prefers-reduced-motion: reduce keeps the ENDING and drops the breath. The
  page is simply in its held state, and 3.2s later in its settled one.
  Nothing swells.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. MEASURED — IT REALLY STOPS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
Read from the live page after a complete run
(http://127.0.0.1:8137/?duration=3600&speed=60, 60.007 real seconds, t
reaching exactly 1), then re-read 1.5 s later:

  document.getAnimations().length ............ 0
      Not "0 running" — zero animation objects of any kind, CSS animations and
      transitions alike. There is nothing left on the compositor.

  identical over 1.5 s ....................... true
      clock t, #tree data-step, plate base index, both layer opacities, plate
      state and the computed width of #timeline-fill were byte-identical
      across the two reads.

  final state
      clock         t = 1, done = true, its rAF loop cancelled by GrowthClock
                    itself (it is never rescheduled after t reaches 1)
      ASCII         step 512 / 512 — the last recorded growth step
      plate         frame 599 at opacity 1, top layer transparent: frame 599
                    alone, unmixed, not a blend
      body          data-phase="bloom" data-state="held" data-settled="true"
      rail          seed/sapling/branching/mature done, bloom active
      bar           inline width "100%", aria-valuenow "100.0"
      readout       "t+60:00 / 60:00",  "seed 42 - bloomed - held"
      headers       "step 512 / 512"    "frame 600 / 600"

  no loop, no reset
      Across the whole 3499-tick recorded run the ASCII step never decreased
      and the plate frame never decreased (see sync-delta.txt section 2).
      There is no code path that re-seeds, rewinds or replays: the ASCII tree
      is grown once at mount and `t` only selects how many of that single
      run's steps have been applied, and the clock clamps t at 1 and cancels
      its loop there.

  network
      At t = 1 the plate's prefetch window ends at the last frame, so once 599
      is on screen nothing further is requested.

  Screenshots
      full-page-t1.00-bloom-settling.jpg   during the breath
      full-page-t1.00-bloom-held.jpg       after data-settled, at rest
      full-page-t1.00-guides.jpg           the same moment with the two
                                           measured landmark lines up


-------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. WHOLE-PAGE POLISH IN THIS PASS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
The two panels had to read as two views of ONE specimen rather than two
widgets. Four changes, all in index.html and src/styles.css:

  THE FOLD (#seam). The divider was a green hairline with a green bloom
  spilling rightward, which read as the left panel bleeding onto the right
  one. It is now a NEUTRAL hairline — neither the terminal's phosphor nor the
  studio's cool white — with the light carried by a separate gradient that is
  dark at top and bottom and brightest across the middle band, the band both
  trees occupy. The eye crosses between the panels where there is something to
  compare.

  THE GROUND (#ground-line). One hairline, drawn across the full width at
  --guide-rim: the exact y that fitPlate() solved so the ASCII pot's lip and
  the rendered pot's rim land on the same row. It runs unbroken from the left
  panel, through the seam, to the right, so the two trees are SEEN to be
  standing on one surface. It is deliberately at the threshold of visibility:
  you notice it only after you notice that both pots sit on it. Measured on
  the shipped page: ground line 576.800 px, ASCII rim 576.800 px, plate rim
  576.800 px.

  THE HEADERS. Both now read FORMAT - LIVE COUNTER in the same type at the
  same gutter: "80 x 40 - step 307 / 512" and "900 x 1200 - frame 360 / 600".
  This replaced a pixel-metrics debug string ("80 x 40 - 6.83x14px") that
  meant nothing to a viewer; that measurement now rides along only when the
  alignment guides are up. The counters are the piece's whole thesis in two
  numbers — one clock, two representations — and they cross their totals
  together at t = 1. Zero-padded and tabular, so the right-aligned header
  never jitters as they climb.

  THE EMPTY UPPER REGION. It is not filled and it is not cropped. cbonsai
  plants its trunk at row 35 and rows 0-18 are permanently blank (BRIEF, M3
  composition note), and the plate is framed to match — so the headroom above
  the specimen is now EXACTLY equal on both sides, to the pixel, which is what
  makes it read as composition rather than as unused area. The header
  counters give the region something to carry; the vignette and the ground
  line give it a floor.

  One defect was found and fixed while doing this. The shell subscribes to the
  clock BEFORE terminal.js and panel.js do — they are separate module scripts,
  evaluated after the inline one — so inside render() the panels have not yet
  been handed the current frame and #tree's data-step still carried the
  PREVIOUS one. At 60Hz that is invisible; at t = 1 the clock stops and the
  header would have sat on the second-to-last step forever, in the one
  screenshot that matters. The counters are now read in a microtask queued
  from render(), which runs once the clock's whole emit loop has unwound —
  same frame, both panels painted.
